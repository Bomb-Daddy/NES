## Comparison assumptions

All reallocations occur before the Spring1175 season ends. “Reassign” moves ten Troops to the selected occupation: drafted population stays103 and Troops become51. “Hire” spends120 Gold for ten troops at the verified Spring quote12.0, then moves those ten troops into the selected occupation: existing military strength remains61 troops, drafted total becomes113. Hiring also dilutes Arms, Skill and Morale by original source formulas; the modeled seed produces Morale111/112 and consequently lower Spring tax receipts. Hired people do not come out of the resident reserve.

Food purchase occurs in Summer1175 through China, whose Food offer and availability are present in the actual captured masks. Quote1.9 buys20 Food for38 Gold. China does **not** offer Food in the supplied Spring baseline, so a supposed Spring China purchase at2.0 is not used. A conditional extra-Fur sale occurs in Autumn through China, whose actual presence and Fur-buying mask are enabled. Quote1.7 sells20 extra Fur for34 whole Gold. Availability and quotes are conditioned on the captured path; changing commands can change RNG scheduling and later diplomatic or event outcomes.

The comparison holds the observed Rate schedule, specialtyFur, Tax35 and no adverse events fixed. It invokes the known economic operations in verified source order, including history accumulation before harvest and Spring food before payroll. It does not simulate political outcomes, natural future random scheduling, battles, save/cold-load, family events or an entire original-game season dispatcher. Gross ability debits are counted; unverified later ability recovery is not inferred. All plans remain affordable and within the captured three-orders-per-player-season budget, even before hypothetical recovery.

## Caps, shortages and limits that change the comparison

The production histories and stocks cap at999. Spring Gold caps after receipts and payroll. Sales additionally cap quantity by `floor(10*(999-Gold)/sellerTenths)`. These are executed by original source routines and explicitly tested:

- Start Food980: both baseline harvest and extra Foodworker harvest saturate999. The added first-harvest Food retained is0, so the uncapped+20 benefit disappears.
- Start Fur980: baseline harvest yields998, extra Artisans yield999. Only1 marginal Fur is retained; conditional sale credits1 Gold instead of34.
- Start Gold990: a quote1.7 sale can accept only5 extra Fur at that moment; it cannot freely realize the entire20-unit increment. The test excludes the unexplained+10 event in this synthetic cap state.
- Economy999 with Town40 already maintains the999 cap; adding ten more Town workers produces no additional Economy at that update. Original-source replay confirms both40 and50 staff end999.
- Food shortage reduces each drafted occupation before tax/payroll; payroll must then use surviving Troops. A zero-Food negative control executes the source shortage path and removes the drafted pool before annual under20 growth. The ordinary table avoids shortage because baseline reserves are ample.

This prevents conclusions such as “every extra worker always earns the same amount,” “production is immediately spendable” or “higher displayed stock is cash profit.” The findings describe original rules and this specified state; they introduce no custom balancing recommendation.
